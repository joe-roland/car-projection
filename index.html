Taxes (illustrative)

Tax results here are scenarios, not a tax return. The before-tax comparison is the main result. To see an after-tax illustration, enter a savings rate from his tax preparer, pick each car's deduction method, then switch on the tax box at the top.

How the tax savings work

1. Eligible business travel can be deducted. If he's a self-employed agent, the business part of his travel costs can reduce his taxable business income. This needs eligible business travel and records. Commuting from home to a regular office does not count. If he's an employee, unreimbursed expenses follow different rules and may not be deductible.

2. A deduction saves only part of a dollar. How much depends on his whole tax picture: income level, filing status, self-employment tax limits and California rules. That's why the savings rate is entered from his tax preparer, not calculated here.

3. For a car, there are two IRS methods, and the choice has rules.

  • Standard mileage: business miles × the IRS rate, plus business-trip parking and tolls, plus the business share of loan interest for an owned car. Gas, insurance, repairs, lease payments and depreciation are covered by the rate and can't be added on top.
  • Actual expenses: the business share of gas, insurance, repairs, registration and loan interest (or lease payments), plus business-trip parking and tolls, plus allowable depreciation for an owned car.
  • For an owned car, standard mileage generally must be used in the first year to keep the option of switching later. For a leased car, the method chosen generally applies for the whole lease. He should decide with his tax preparer.

4. Rides: the eligible business share of work rides and rentals is a business travel expense. Personal rides are not deductible.

5. Never deductible: traffic and parking tickets, personal and commuting parking, and home parking unless his preparer says otherwise.

Electric cars: charging costs count in actual expenses the same way gas does. With standard mileage, the per-mile rate covers charging, so it can't be added on top. Ask his preparer whether a home charger qualifies for any deduction or credit; this calculator doesn't count one.

Records he'll need: a mileage log (date, miles, client or property) and receipts. Ride apps keep trip history.
